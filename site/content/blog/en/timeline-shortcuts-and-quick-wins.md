> **Applies to:** Clipey 1.15. Integrated controls across timeline and editor panels.

To edit faster with timeline shortcuts in Clipey, hold Shift while dragging or trimming to temporarily disable snapping, and right-click clips or modifier pills to access quick actions like Split / Cut, Duplicate, and Delete. Press M at the playhead to place named Timeline markers for rapid navigation without altering video cuts. When working with complex views, double-click any panel divider to instantly reset it to its default size.

## Drag without snapping with Shift

1. Select the clip or trim you want to adjust and begin dragging.
2. While holding the mouse button, hold **Shift** to temporarily bypass snapping. The visual indicator **Snapping disabled (Shift)** appears while the key is pressed.
3. Drop the item where you want it, then release Shift. Snapping returns without changing a persistent setting.

This modifier applies to that interaction; it does not turn snapping off for the whole project. Use it when an item keeps sticking to a nearby edge and you need to place it between references. Pressing Shift after the drag is over will not move the item, so repeat the adjustment while holding the key.

## Open actions on the clip

The context menu gathers existing actions for clips and modifier pills, including **Split / Cut**, **Duplicate**, **Delete**, **Edit start and end**, **Add Zoom** and **Add Speed**. Right-click an element to open the menu and check the shortcut badges beside commands. They help you discover an action before memorising its key combination.

The menu is fully keyboard accessible: select an item and press **Shift+F10** or the context-menu key. Press **Esc** to close it; focus returns directly to the clip so you can keep navigating without losing your place. Available actions adapt dynamically to the selected element.

## Add markers for review points

1. Move the playhead to the moment you want to revisit.
2. Press **M** to trigger **Add marker** at that position.
3. Open **Timeline markers** to jump back while reviewing. You can use **Rename marker**, choose a new shade with **Marker color** or select **Delete marker**.

Markers identify positions in the project; they do not cut, hide or change footage. Use names such as “check title” or “verify audio” to make the list a review agenda. If a section moves after editing, check that its marker still points to the moment you intended to save.

## Reset a panel's size

If a panel becomes too narrow after resizing the divider, double-click the divider line. This triggers **Reset to default size**, restoring standard workspace proportions without manual dragging.

## Tips

1. Hold Shift only while dragging or trimming past an unwanted snap; it does not change a lasting preference.
2. Check the menu's shortcut badges before memorising commands. Actions depend on the selected clip or modifier.
3. Name markers for the next review task, not only for what is visible at that moment.
4. Remove stale markers so the list stays useful on longer projects.
5. If the inspector has lost room, try double-clicking the divider before repeatedly resizing panels.

## Limitations

Shift bypasses snapping only during the active interaction; it does not change the global timeline setting. The context menu displays actions relevant to the clicked item rather than a static list. Markers store navigation positions and do not execute cuts or splits.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I temporarily disable magnetic snapping on the timeline?
Hold down the Shift key while dragging a clip or adjusting a trim edge. The visual indicator "Snapping disabled (Shift)" appears on screen, and normal snapping resumes the moment you release the key.

### What happens when I add a marker on the timeline?
Pressing M places a named marker at the playhead position for quick navigation and review notes. Markers store reference times in the project and do not split or modify video clips.

### How can I reset the size of an editor panel if it was resized?
Double-click directly on the divider line between the timeline and neighboring panels to reset the workspace to its default proportions.
